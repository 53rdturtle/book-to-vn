Chapter 1: The Meeting

The sky over Millhaven was just beginning to pale when Clara arrived at the old bookshop. She had not slept. In her pocket was a note written in a hand she did not recognize, asking her to come at dawn and to tell no one.

A young man was already waiting at the door. He wore a coat too large for him and carried a brass key on a loop of string.

"You came," he said. "I wasn't sure you would."

"Your note wasn't exactly polite," Clara replied. "Who are you?"

"My name is Edwin. I keep this shop." He held up the key. "Come back at nine tonight. I'll explain everything. For now, just take this — and don't lose it."


Chapter 2: The Ledger

At nine, the shop was quiet and smelled of old paper and cedar. Edwin sat at a long table with a leather ledger open in front of him. Clara set the brass key down beside it.

"Every person who opens that door," Edwin said, "leaves a story behind. Something they want to forget, or something they cannot."

Clara looked at the ledger. The last entry was written in her mother's handwriting.

"She was here," she whispered.

"Three years ago," Edwin said. "She started a story and could not finish it. She asked me to keep the page open until her daughter came."


Chapter 3: The Ending

Clara sat down slowly. The page under her hand was her mother's, and it stopped mid-sentence. The ink at the end was faded but not blurred, as though the writer had put the pen down carefully.

"Did she say what the ending should be?" Clara asked.

Edwin shook his head. "She said only that you would know when the time came."

Clara picked up the pen. For a long moment she did not write. Then, in a hand very like her mother's, she finished the sentence and closed the ledger.

Edwin bowed his head, just slightly. "Thank you, Clara. The story is whole now."
